[[Vorathic Family/Grave Tongue/index|Grave Tongue]] uses a abuguida/syllabary for its orthography. Due to the large (C)(C)(C)V(C)(C) syllable struction, resulting in over 22 million combinations, the glyphs are made in a procedural predicable way. First we will look at the overall structure of the glyphs then describe the components that make it up.

### General Glyph Structure

A glyph is made of 3 general regions. Each one represents the onest, vowel, or coda. To indicate each region a vertical line is drawn as the centre of the glyph. The left side of the line represents the onset. The right side represents the coda. The vowel is marked using a small diacritic like mark at the bottom of the line. Within these regions long horizontal lines are draw to separate out subsections for the consonants. These subsections are only createdd if needed for the syllable. For example a syllable with only 1 onset and no coda consonants would not have any horizontal lines as no separation of consonants is needed.

### Vowel Diacritics

Since [[Vorathic Family/Grave Tongue/index|Grave Tongue]] evolved from [[Vorathic Family/Vorathic/index|Vorathic]]'s 3 vowel system there are 3 base vowel marks that combine to form the 4 other vowels. This is because the vowels evolved from diphlong environments.

| Sound | Diacritic                                                                                     |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| o     | A small line going forward in a 45 degree angle.                                              |
| u     | A small line going backward in a 45 degree angle.                                             |
| a     | This is kind of like a small upside down T shape. Made from a vertical and a horizonatl line. |
| ɔ     | An a diacritic and an o diacritic combined.                                                   |
| aw    | An a diacritic and an u diacritic combined.                                                   |
| wa    | All 3 diacritics combined.                                                                    |
| wo    | An u diacritic and an o diacritic combined.                                                   |

### Consonant Symbols

Since a consonant form appears in a subsection of the larger glyph it can appear with a long top and/or bottom horizontal line. This is typical omitted unless it is needed to separate subsections as described in the General Glyph Structure section. The base vertical line is always included. The descriptions below are for an onset consonant. When creating a coda mirror the consonant symbol.

Consonant symbols can be described in a 7-segment display like way. I have named each possible piece of the symbols below and created a table of how they form.
a) a long vertical line on the far left side of the glyph. If there were a top and bottom horizontal line this would form a square shape.
b) a long vertical line going down the centre of the subsection.
c) the topmost of two horizontal lines that go from the rightmost vertical line to the vertical centre of the subsection. These divide the subsection into thirds.
d) the bottomost of the two horizontal lines described in c.
e) a small horizontal line going from the centre of the rightmost vetical line to the centre of the subsection.
f) the continuation of the line from e all the way to the leftmost side of the subsection

A means a labial
B means an alveolar
No A/B means velar

C means fricative
D means approximate
E means nasal
No C/D/E means stop
F means voicing
